Visualization

Figures and tidy data from steady-state sweeps.

Build a Sweep from a list of case dicts and the matching list of result frames (State.to_dataframe() outputs, optionally with threshold rows and uq_attach bands), declare the limits the design must respect, then ask it a question (profile, field, reduce, required) and plot the answer:

cases_ = cases(power=[2e6, 4e6, 6e6], mdot=[0.2, 0.3], wall_law=["Dittus", "regime"])
cases_, frames = run_cases(model, cases_)
sweep = Sweep(cases_, frames, agr,
              labels={"power": ("Power", "MW", 1e-6)},
              limits={"CHFR": at_least(1.3), "T_clad": at_most(900.0)})
print(sweep)
sweep.profile("T_cool", where=dict(power=4e6, mdot=0.3, wall_law="regime")).plot()
sweep.reduce("CHFR", "min", versus="power").plot(color="mdot", style="wall_law")
sweep.reduce("CHFR", "min", versus="power", where=dict(wall_law="regime")).plot(crossing="mark")
sweep.reduce("CHFR", "min", versus=("power", "mdot")).plot(col="wall_law", limit="shade")
sweep.required(["CHFR", "T_clad"], solve_for="power", versus="mdot", limiting=True).plot()

Every answer carries frame, a tidy DataFrame with lower, value and upper columns from the uncertainty envelopes, and plot draws it with generated labels and legends under the STREAM style. A declared limit is drawn on every figure that shows its quantity, and limiting=True collapses several quantities or calculations to the one that binds.

class Sweep(cases, frames, agr, labels=None, limits=None, fill_holes=False)[source]

Cases, result frames and the aggregator they came from.

Ask print(sweep) for the parameters and their levels, the quantities of each calculation, the holes and the limits.

Parameters:

  • cases – One dict per case, all with the same keys. A value is a number, or a string for a parameter that is a choice (a wall law, a correlation); each parameter is one or the other in every case. A choice can colour, style or panel a figure, never be an axis.

  • frames – One State.to_dataframe() result per case, or None for a case whose solve failed (a hole).

  • agr – The aggregator, static across the sweep, used for coordinates.

  • labels – Display names, units and scales, merged over the module defaults.

  • limits –

    The limits the design must respect, one per quantity:

    limits={"CHFR": at_least(1.3), "T_clad": at_most(900.0)}

    A declared limit is drawn on every figure that shows the quantity, gives required its target and limiting=True its meaning.

  • fill_holes – True fills a hole when, along a numeric parameter and with every other parameter equal, the grid levels immediately below and above it are solved cases; the fill interpolates linearly between the two (the mean over the parameters that bracket it). A hole that no numeric parameter brackets this way (at the edge of the sweep, or next to another hole, along every one of them) stays a hole. Filled rows carry filled=True in every frame, draw with an open marker (a field ends its subtitle with , interpolated) and are listed in filled. Off by default because an interpolated case is a guess.

reduce(quantity, reducer, versus=None, of=None, where=None, axis=None, nearest=False, sigma=1.0, uncertainty=True, limiting=False)[source]

Collapse a quantity per case and plot it against a parameter, against two parameters as a map, or along one field axis.

sweep.reduce("CHFR", "min", versus="power")
sweep.reduce("CHFR", "min", versus="power").plot(color="mdot")
sweep.reduce("CHFR", "min", versus=("power", "mdot")).plot()
sweep.reduce("T", "max", axis="x")
sweep.reduce("CHFR", "min", versus="power", of=channels, limiting=True)
Parameters:

  • quantity – One name or a list of names, all of one unit.

  • reducer –

    How the cells of each case are collapsed:

    • "min": the smallest cell value;

    • "max": the largest cell value;

    • "mean": the mean weighted by cell size;

    • "where_min": the coordinate of the smallest value;

    • "where_max": the coordinate of the largest value;

    • at(z=...), at(x=...) or at(cell=...): the value of one cell;

    • a callable f(values, coords): whatever it returns.

    A scalar quantity is taken as it is. A where_* reducer gives a location and a callable an unknown value, so their results carry no limit: limit="line" draws nothing, and limit="shade" or limiting=True raises.

  • versus – A numeric parameter for the x axis, or a pair of two numeric parameters (a tuple or a list) for a map (filled contours, the limit as a contour). When the map’s reducer is the limit’s own (min for at_least, max for at_most), its limit contour is the curve required(solve_for=versus[0], versus=versus[1]) draws.

  • of – The calculation(s) that own the quantity, when ambiguous.

  • where – Parameters fixed at a grid level.

  • axis –

    Collapse a field along one axis and keep a profile along the other, instead of versus:

    • "z": collapse along z, a profile along x;

    • "x": collapse along x, a profile along z.

  • nearest – Snap where values to the nearest grid level instead of raising.

  • sigma – The multiple of stat in the envelopes; sys is added as it is.

  • uncertainty – False drops the envelopes; lower and upper equal value.

  • limiting – True keeps, per case, only the calculation (and quantity) that binds: the smallest reduced value for a limit that is at_least, the largest for at_most. The frame names it in calculation and quantity. Needs every quantity’s limit declared, all with one side and one value: values are compared as they are, so across quantities it is meaningful only when they share a limit value; use required(..., limiting=True) for quantities with different limits.

required(quantity, equals=None, solve_for=None, versus=None, reduce=None, of=None, at=None, where=None, method='linear', nearest=False, sigma=1.0, uncertainty=True, limiting=False)[source]

The value of solve_for at which the reduced quantity meets its limit, against versus.

Each quantity is reduced over the mesh per case, the curve along solve_for is walked at every level of versus (and of every other free parameter), and the crossing is interpolated:

sweep.required("CHFR", solve_for="power", versus="mdot")
sweep.required("CHFR", equals=1.5, solve_for="power", versus="mdot")
sweep.required(["CHFR", "OSVR"], solve_for="power", versus="mdot")
sweep.required(["CHFR", "OSVR"], equals={"CHFR": 1.3, "OSVR": 1.1}, solve_for="power", versus="mdot")
sweep.required(["CHFR", "OSVR"], of=channels, solve_for="power", versus="mdot", limiting=True)
Parameters:

  • quantity – One name or a list of names. Each is walked on its own, so their units may differ.

  • equals – The target. None (the default) uses each quantity’s declared limit; a number applies to every quantity; a quantity name walks the difference to that quantity (equals="q_CHF"); a dict gives each quantity its own.

  • solve_for – Required. The parameter to walk along. Numeric.

  • versus – Required. The parameter on the x axis. Numeric.

  • reduce – The mesh reducer, any value reduce() takes. None uses min for a limit that is at_least and max for one that is at_most (min without a declared limit); one reducer applies to every quantity; a dict gives each its own.

  • of – The calculation(s) that own the quantity, when ambiguous.

  • at – Parameters fixed at a value, on or off the grid; off the grid, the walk is interpolated between the two neighbouring levels.

  • where – Parameters fixed at a grid level.

  • method –

    How the crossing is interpolated between grid points:

    • "linear": straight lines between them (the default);

    • "pchip": a monotone cubic through them.

  • nearest – Snap where values to the nearest grid level instead of raising.

  • sigma – The multiple of stat in the envelopes; sys is added as it is.

  • uncertainty – False drops the envelopes; lower and upper equal value.

  • limiting – True keeps, at each level of versus (and of every other free parameter), only the quantity and the calculation that bind, the smallest required value when the safe side lies below it and the largest when above. A quantity safe everywhere at that level does not bind there; one safe nowhere makes the level infeasible (its value is NaN). The frame names the binder in quantity and calculation, and the plot colours the markers by it. Needs every quantity’s limit declared, and the quantities that cross at one level safe on the same side of their crossings. What is compared is the required value of solve_for, so quantities of different units and limits combine.

Returns:

A curve whose frame has one row per crossing with lower, value, upper (the walked envelopes), filled (whether the crossing rests on an interpolated case) and safe, where the quantity is safe:

  • "below": below the crossing, in solve_for;

  • "above": above it;

  • "everywhere": the walk never reaches the target and every solved point is safe;

  • "nowhere": the walk never reaches the target and every solved point is unsafe;

  • None: no limit is declared for the quantity.

Return type:

Result

class Result(kind, frame, x, dims, numeric, fixed, calculation, labels, banded, reducer=None, ylabel_source=(), sigma=1.0, walk_reducer=None, walk_reducers=<factory>, level_space=<factory>, coordinate=None, target=None, targets=<factory>, walk_quantity=None, limits=<factory>, safe_side=None, binding=False, y=None, holes=<factory>, limit_note=None, reducer_at=None, walk_reducers_at=<factory>)[source]

The answer to a question asked of a Sweep, ready to plot.

kind is one of:

  • "profile": a quantity along a mesh coordinate, from profile or reduce(axis=...);

  • "curve": a value against a parameter, from reduce or required;

  • "map": a value over two parameters, from reduce with a pair versus;

  • "field": a quantity over a two axis mesh, from field.

The fields a user reads:

  • frame: the tidy DataFrame, one row per drawn point, with the dimension columns, the x column, lower, value, upper and filled;

  • x: the column on the x axis;

  • y: the parameter on the y axis of a map, else None;

  • dims: the dimensions that vary, with their levels;

  • fixed: the parameters that do not vary, with their values;

  • calculation: the calculation drawn, when there is one;

  • limits: the declared limits of the quantities drawn, when the values are the quantity’s own;

  • safe_side: for required, the side of the crossing where every quantity is safe, when it is one;

  • binding: whether limiting=True collapsed the result;

  • targets: for required, the target walked for each quantity;

  • walk_reducers: for required, the mesh reducer of each quantity;

  • holes: the unsolved cases of the selection left unfilled;

  • limit_note: why the result carries no limit, for a where_* or custom reducer, else None;

  • reducer_at: the at reducer of a reduce result, so a figure prints its position with the labels it is drawn under, else None;

  • walk_reducers_at: for required, the at reducer of each quantity walked with one.

A binding frame carries a binder column naming the quantity (and quantity of calculation when calculations varied) that binds at each point. plot draws the result.
