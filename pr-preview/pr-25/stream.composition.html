MTR Geometry

MTR: Material Test Reactors

In this context, MTR refers to a typical type of fuels, which comprise of several rectangular parallel plates in each rod, with coolant flowing between the plates.

The combination of calculations into an MTR-like setup, containing:

  1. Fuel Plates

  2. Coolant (T, incompressible scheme) channels on both sides.

The created objects are CalculationGraph objects which may be combined to eventually create an executable scheme.

Report if \(\sum_{loop}\Delta p(\dot{m}=0) \neq 0\) for any loop

Usually, if there are no flows at all and thermally equal, total pressure drops in loops should be trivially zero. If that’s not the case, it is probably due to gravity pressures of differing heights.

Steady-state guesses

Steady-state initial guesses for a flow network wall-coupled to fuel plates.

One call, guess_steady_state, produces a guess for the whole system that solve_steady can start from. It works in three stages:

  1. decompose splits the aggregator into blocks: the hydraulic block (the Kirchhoff and everything on its flow graph), one thermal cluster per group of wall-coupled Fuel plates and ChannelAndContacts channels, and the point-kinetics node that supplies fuel power.

  2. seed_steady_state seeds every block in closed form, without solving: flows from what is known and the current law, temperatures by walking the network in flow direction with each heated channel adding its power, pressures from guess_hydraulic_steady_state, walls and fuel interiors from the local heat flux, and kinetics from point_kinetics_steady_state.

  3. The seed is refined by solving one block at a time with the rest of the system frozen (solve_block): the thermal clusters, then the hydraulics, a few times over.

Use this module for a system that couples a flow network to heated plates, where the flows set the temperatures and the temperatures feed back into the pressure drops. guess_steady_state covers the hydraulic part alone and needs every flow and one temperature from the caller; the helpers in stream.composition.subsystems cover a single plate or a single kinetics node. guess_steady_state combines them, completes the flows it is not given, and reconciles the blocks with each other.

For a loop of one heated plate, where channel is a ChannelAndContacts, fuel its Fuel and top, bottom two Junction nodes:

fg = FlowGraph(
    flow_edge((top, bottom), channel),
    flow_edge((bottom, top), Pump(mdot0=0.5, name="Pump"), HeatExchanger(outlet=40.0, name="HX")),
    abs_pressure_comps=[channel],
    reference_node=(top, 2e5),
)
agr = fg.aggregator + symmetric_plate(channel, fuel, {fuel: {"power": 1e3}}).to_aggregator()
y = agr.solve_steady(guess_steady_state(agr))

Limitations: an aggregator holds at most one Kirchhoff (with any number of loops on it) and at most one kinetics node. Fuels are heated through the T_left and T_right walls of a Channel only. A fuel driven by a kinetics node needs its power in powers. A heated channel on a loop that nothing drives needs a signed flow in flows. Calculations the seed does not recognise go through overrides.

seed_steady_state(agr, k=None, *, flows=None, temperature=None, powers=None, overrides=None, t=0.0, strategy=None, walk_passes=50, walk_tolerance=1e-06)[source]

A closed-form steady-state guess for a flow network wall-coupled to fuel plates.

Nothing is solved. Flows come from what is known (flows, a pump’s fixed flow, a closed flapper) and a minimum-norm completion of the current law; temperatures are walked around the network in flow direction with each heated channel adding its power over flow times heat capacity; pressures come from guess_hydraulic_steady_state at those temperatures; walls sit at coolant plus flux over a floored heat transfer coefficient, and fuel interiors are tiled from their walls. Funcs win over walked values, as they do in the Aggregator.

A system without a Kirchhoff (plates that take their coolant from funcs, as symmetric_plate builds them) is seeded channel by channel: the flow from flows or the channel’s mdot func, the inlet from its Tin func (Tin_minus for reversed flow), the absolute pressure from its p_abs func (1e5 Pa without one), and the channel’s own pressure from its pressure drop at the seeded temperatures.

Parameters:

  • agr (Aggregator) – The full system.

  • k (Kirchhoff or None) – The flow solver that sits in agr. If None, the Kirchhoff of agr is used.

  • flows (Mapping[Calculation | str, KgPerS] or None) – Known flows by component or name. Needed only where the network cannot fix a flow itself: a head-driven pump, or a pump-free loop (pass a small signed flow). Without a Kirchhoff, a channel’s entry here wins over its mdot func.

  • temperature (Celsius or None) – Reference temperature. Required when the network has no heat exchanger and no pinned inlet; otherwise it only sets the walk’s start. Without a Kirchhoff, it is the inlet of a channel that has no Tin or Tin_minus func.

  • powers (Mapping[Calculation | str, Watt] or None) – Fuel powers by fuel or name. Override a fuel’s power func; required for a fuel driven by a kinetics node.

  • overrides (DictState or None) – Merged last, for calculations the seed does not recognise; a calculation named here is not reported as unrecognised.

  • t (Second) – Time at which funcs are evaluated.

  • strategy (HydraulicStrategyMap or None) – Per-component pressure-drop laws for components the hydraulic guesser does not know.

  • walk_passes (int) – The seed walks the network in flow direction, computing each component’s outlet from its inlet and mixing the arrivals at every junction, and repeats the pass until no temperature moves by more than walk_tolerance, at most walk_passes times. A loop settles in a few passes. Default 50.

  • walk_tolerance (Celsius) – The largest temperature change that ends the walk. Default 1e-6.

Return type:

State

Raises:

  • MissingFlowError – A heated channel whose flow nothing fixes.

  • MissingPowerError – A fuel whose steady power is unknown.

  • ValueError – Two known flows on one edge disagree, no temperature source and no temperature, or the Kirchhoff or the kinetics node is ambiguous.

solve_block(agr, nodes, state, t=0.0, **solver_options)[source]

Solve a subset of an aggregator’s calculations with the rest of the system frozen.

Every input a block calculation receives from outside the block is read from state and handed to a throwaway sub-aggregator as a constant func; the block’s own funcs are kept and take precedence. The sub-aggregator is solved with solve_steady.

Parameters:

  • agr (Aggregator) – The full system.

  • nodes (Iterable[Calculation]) – The calculations to solve. Every other calculation is frozen.

  • state (DictState or Array1D) – The current state of the whole system; the block starts from its own part of it.

  • t (Second) – Time at which the block’s own funcs and the frozen boundary values are evaluated, and the time stamp of the returned State.

  • solver_options – Forwarded to solve_steady.

Returns:

The solved block, calculations of the block only.

Return type:

State

Raises:

  • ValueError – If a block calculation takes one variable partly from inside and partly from outside the block, or a Junction sits on the boundary.

  • KeyError – If state lacks a calculation of agr.

  • AlgRuntimeError – If the block does not solve.

maximally_coupled(*calculations, exclude=None)[source]

Connect calculations into an CalculationGraph as fully as possible through inspection. This is an opt-out way of constructing such connections, if you will.

Parameters:

  • calculations (Calculation) – The calculations to be connected

  • exclude (Callable[[str], bool], optional) – An exclusion strategy for variable names, that is True values are omitted.

Returns:

Return type:

CalculationGraph

Examples

>>> from stream.composition import Calculation_factory as factory
>>> Addition = factory(lambda y, *, x: y + x, [False], dict(y=0))
>>> Multiplication = factory(lambda x, *, y: x * y, [False], dict(x=0))
>>> add, mult = Addition(name="+"), Multiplication("*")
>>> maximally_coupled(add, mult).to_aggregator().external
{*: {'y': {+: 0}}, +: {'x': {*: 1}}}
>>> maximally_coupled(add, mult, exclude=lambda s: s=="x").to_aggregator().external
