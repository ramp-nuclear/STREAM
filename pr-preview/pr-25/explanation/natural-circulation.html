Natural circulation and flow reversal

A pool reactor that loses its pumps keeps its core cooled by buoyancy: the water in the heated channels is lighter than the water around them, and the difference in weight drives a flow with no pump in the loop. This page explains where that head comes from, why a core cooled downward reverses, what a reversal asks of the model, and why the wall law matters most once the forced flow is gone. Both Pumps Trip: Coastdown, Trip, Flapper, Natural Convection shows the whole sequence on a pool reactor, and Get a steady solve to converge solves a small natural-circulation loop.

Where the head comes from

Around a closed loop the hot column of water pushes up and the cold one pushes down, and the only pressure source left when the pumps are gone is that difference:

\[\Delta p_b = g\,\Delta h\,(\rho_c - \rho_h)\]

It is small: in the both-pumps case the hot plate’s buoyancy head at the end of the hour is about 39 Pa, against about 6807 Pa for the weight of its 0.7 m column. The loop settles at the flow where friction spends that head, which is why a pool reactor offers buoyancy a short path: the flapper joins the plenum below the core to the pool beside it, so the natural-circulation loop is the core, the pool and the flapper, not the pump legs and the heat exchanger.

Why the core reverses

In forced flow the pumps drive the water down through the core, and each heated channel’s buoyancy pushes up against them as a small correction. As the flywheels run down, the downward head falls below the buoyancy of the hottest channels, their flow passes through zero and turns upward.

Parallel channels do not reverse together. The one with the most power per unit flow has the most buoyancy and turns first, and for as long as some channels have turned and others have not, the plena carry the difference: water goes down the cold channels and up the hot ones, a recirculation inside the core that no single channel describes. Both Pumps Trip: Coastdown, Trip, Flapper, Natural Convection times each group’s reversal.

What a reversal does to the model

A channel is differenced upwind, so its inlet is the end the water comes from. When the flow reverses, the inlet becomes the outlet, and every component needs the temperature it would be fed from the other side. That is what Tin and Tin_minus are: Tin is the temperature from the neighbour upstream along the drawn edge, Tin_minus from the neighbour downstream, and directed_Tin picks one by the sign of the flow, blending the two within 1e-3 kg/s of zero so that the residual has no jump at the reversal. A Junction mixes its streams the same way: each stream’s weight is a smooth positive part of its flow into the junction, so a stream that turns to leave the junction stops feeding it heat, to within that smoothing.

Two consequences follow for how a loop is drawn. A channel is always modelled as running downward along its edge: its physics reads only the cell lengths of z_boundaries, so reversing the boundaries builds the same channel when the cells are uniform and the same cells in reverse order when they are not, and a leg that rises is a Gravity with a negative disposition. And a gravity leg weighs the water it is fed: with one heat exchanger at one end, reversed flow reaches it hot from the other end and the loop loses the cold column it circulates on. A HeatExchanger sets its outlet temperature whatever its inlet, so placing one on each side of the leg keeps the column cold whichever way the water runs.

The wall law at low flow

A purely turbulent correlation loses the plate’s heat transfer as the flow dies; A Pool Reactor for Loss-of-Flow Analysis measures what that costs the plate. The regime-dependent law keeps a floor by construction. Its forced part interpolates on the Reynolds number between a laminar and a turbulent correlation; natural convection is then added to it by superposition, the cube root of the sum of the cubes, so buoyancy only ever raises the coefficient; and once the through-flow no longer renews the water in the channel, measured by the Graetz number, the law hands over to the natural-convection value of a stagnant channel. Every switch reads the flow, never the wall superheat the coefficient feeds back into, so the coefficient stays finite through a reversal. It is the default of stream.examples.lofa.

The flows and their signs are also what a natural-circulation steady solve needs from its guess; Steady states and guesses explains why, and Sign conventions collects the signs.
