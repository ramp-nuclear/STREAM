Sign conventions

Every sign in a loop follows from a few rules about the drawn edges. This page states the rules, then lists the names that carry a sign, an owner or a mirror. Wire a flow network wires a loop under them, and Building the Pool Reactor Loop draws the pool reactor by them.

The rules

A flow’s sign is along the drawn edge. flow_edge places its components in series from the edge’s tail to its head, and a positive flow runs that way. Nothing else fixes a direction: a reversed edge that still closes its loop builds a different plant, with its pump pushing the other way.

The pressure is a gain. Each component’s pressure is the rise from its inlet to its outlet along the edge, and around every loop the gains sum to zero. A Pump holds its head, positive; a Resistor, a friction element or a check valve is negative for forward flow; an Inertia is minus its inertance times the flow’s derivative.

Down is positive. A Gravity returns \(\rho g \Delta h\) for a disposition \(\Delta h\), a gain for a positive one, so a positive disposition is a drop in height along the edge and a riser takes a negative one. pressure_diff, the channel’s pressure law, takes positive flow as downward, and a channel’s physics reads only the cell lengths of its z_boundaries: a channel is always modelled as running down its edge, so heated channels are drawn top to bottom and a rising leg is written with a Gravity, not by reversing the boundaries, which on uneven cells only reverses their order.

Heat flux is positive into the fluid. A channel computes its wall fluxes as the coefficient times the wall temperature less the bulk temperature, so a wall hotter than the water heats it.

Left and right are mirrored across a contact. The consumer names the side it sees and the supplier answers with the face that looks at it: a Fuel asked for T_left answers with its own T_wall_right, and a ChannelAndContacts asked for h_left answers with its own h_right.
