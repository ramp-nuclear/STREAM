Examples package

A pool-reactor loss-of-flow case: a plate core in forced downward flow, a flapper joining the plenum below the core to the bottom of the pool beside it, a decay tank, two pump legs with flywheels and check valves, a heat exchanger, and the pool as the natural-circulation heat sink. Point kinetics with temperature feedback and a low-flow trip drive the power.

class Record(status, error, residual_entry, residual_steady, head, mdot_total, mdot_hot, mdot_normal, mdot_bypass, T_out_hot, T_out_error_max, split_error_max, t_trip, t_rods, t_open, t_valve_A, t_valve_B, t_rev_hot, t_rev_normal, t_rev_low, reversal_in_buoyancy_order, peak_hot, peak_max, peak_fuel_hot, margin, final_hot, final_bypass, final_pool, final_leg_A, final_leg_B, final_flapper, pool_rise, t_last, t_half, t_half_hand)[source]

Outcome of one scenario run: status is completed, saturation or failed.

A valve’s shut time t_valve_A or t_valve_B is the first output sample at which that leg’s flow is below 1 % of the nominal leg flow, so a leg still coasting forward counts as shut. margin is the saturation temperature at the reference pressure at the core top minus the sampled peak coolant temperature; the saturation stop uses the local pressure, so it can fire at a slightly higher local temperature than margin suggests. Reversal and valve times are quantised to the output grid, and the sampled peak moves with the grid (about 0.01 °C between the 901- and 3601-point grids). t_half is the first output time at which the riser flow is below half its initial value, counted from t=0 and so including the t_pumps before the pumps trip; t_half_hand is the flywheel estimate of the time from the pump trip, inertia times nominal leg flow over the solved pump head, in seconds.

energy_budget(agr, K, refs, sol, p)[source]

Energy balance of the whole run, J.

fed_J is the heat the core produced: the point-kinetics power integrated over the output times. hx_removed_J is the heat the exchanger took out: its forward flow times the specific heat times the drop from the J_merge temperature to p.hx_outlet, integrated over the run. stored_J is the rise in internal energy from the first to the last output time of the water in every component with a coolant temperature (its density and specific heat taken at the first output time) and of the fuel plates, each core channel and plate counted as many times as its group has plates; parts_J lists it per component. unaccounted_J is fed_J - hx_removed_J - stored_J, the closure residual: positive when heat the core produced was neither removed by the exchanger nor stored. The balance closes when it is small against fed_J.

loop_pressure_budget(agr, K, refs, sol, loop_names, idx=-1)[source]

Pressure budget of one closed loop at output index idx (the last output by default): the pressure difference of each named component, Pa, and the hot plate’s buoyancy head, Pa.

The loop is walked through the components in the order given. A component’s pressure difference keeps its sign when the walk follows its edge’s positive flow direction and is negated when the walk runs against it. The component pressure differences include their inertia terms, so when every component on the walked edges is named, the entries sum to the loop’s Kirchhoff residual, which the solver holds at zero at every output time, steady or not; the entries show where around the loop the pressure is gained and lost. The buoyancy head, given for comparison, is the gravitational acceleration times the sum over the hot channel’s cells of the density of the water entering from J_plenum minus the cell’s density, times the cell length.

The names must chain edge to edge around a closed loop in that order, otherwise ValueError is raised; components sharing an edge are listed consecutively and take that edge’s sign. At least two names are needed and none may repeat.

energy_budget(agr, K, refs, sol, p)[source]

Energy balance of the whole run, J.

fed_J is the heat the core produced: the point-kinetics power integrated over the output times. hx_removed_J is the heat the exchanger took out: its forward flow times the specific heat times the drop from the J_merge temperature to p.hx_outlet, integrated over the run. stored_J is the rise in internal energy from the first to the last output time of the water in every component with a coolant temperature (its density and specific heat taken at the first output time) and of the fuel plates, each core channel and plate counted as many times as its group has plates; parts_J lists it per component. unaccounted_J is fed_J - hx_removed_J - stored_J, the closure residual: positive when heat the core produced was neither removed by the exchanger nor stored. The balance closes when it is small against fed_J.

loop_pressure_budget(agr, K, refs, sol, loop_names, idx=-1)[source]

Pressure budget of one closed loop at output index idx (the last output by default): the pressure difference of each named component, Pa, and the hot plate’s buoyancy head, Pa.

The loop is walked through the components in the order given. A component’s pressure difference keeps its sign when the walk follows its edge’s positive flow direction and is negated when the walk runs against it. The component pressure differences include their inertia terms, so when every component on the walked edges is named, the entries sum to the loop’s Kirchhoff residual, which the solver holds at zero at every output time, steady or not; the entries show where around the loop the pressure is gained and lost. The buoyancy head, given for comparison, is the gravitational acceleration times the sum over the hot channel’s cells of the density of the water entering from J_plenum minus the cell’s density, times the cell length.

The names must chain edge to edge around a closed loop in that order, otherwise ValueError is raised; components sharing an edge are listed consecutively and take that edge’s sign. At least two names are needed and none may repeat.
